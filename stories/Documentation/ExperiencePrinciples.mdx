## Intent

Mangrove is evolving towards a lighter, slightly softer digital experience. This is a considered refinement of the existing system, not a wholesale redesign. We keep the shared semantics, content structures, accessibility, themes and institutional clarity that make Mangrove dependable, while reducing visual hardness where it does not help people understand or act.

**Clear, calm and approachable** describes the intent. Interfaces should feel authoritative without feeling severe. Observable outcomes include fewer competing boundaries, stable layouts during interaction, clear control affordances, consistent token-driven geometry and visible states in every theme.

This direction supports UNDRR's <LinkTo kind="brand-brand-guidelines" story="docs">brand characteristics</LinkTo> of being knowledgeable, approachable and collaborative.

## Scope and ownership

Mangrove defines shared semantics, accessibility, content structure, interaction behaviour, baseline geometry and themeable capabilities. Themes own colour identity and surface expression within those guardrails. A theme may change radii, fills, typography scale and accent treatments, but it must not weaken the accessible interaction baseline.

Promote repeated behaviour, accessibility, content structure or themeable capability to a shared component. Put property-specific visual expression in documented theme tokens. This keeps Mangrove aligned with its role as a shared foundation rather than a <LinkTo kind="getting-started-about-mangrove" story="docs">visual brand enforcer</LinkTo>.

## Decision order

When principles compete, decide in this order:

1. User need and safety
2. Accessibility and semantic meaning
3. Content comprehension
4. Shared behaviour and compatibility
5. Theme and brand expression
6. Visual polish

Emergency communication, dense data and task completion take priority over a softer appearance.

## Principles

### Keep the foundation, refine the expression

Prefer evolution over replacement. Preserve semantic HTML, established content models, `mg-` class contracts, responsive behaviour, right-to-left support and theme compatibility. Prefer additive tokens and variants. Treat changed defaults as consumer-visible changes, document their migration impact and do not silently override a theme's choices.

### Let content lead

Hierarchy should come from clear typography, spacing and meaningful imagery before decoration. Use readable line lengths and a deliberate rhythm. Use balanced heading wraps where they improve scanning without creating awkward breaks in translated or expanded text. Keep labels and actions concise.

### Soften with restraint

Use token-driven corner radii, lightly tinted surfaces and gentle separation to reduce visual hardness. Softer does not mean pill-shaped by default, borderless everywhere or visually vague. Structural boundaries must remain apparent, especially in forms, tables and dense information.

### Make actions obvious and comfortable

Buttons, links and controls should have consistent geometry, clear states and generous targets. Meet the WCAG 2.2 minimum of 24 by 24 CSS pixels and aim for 44 by 44 where space permits. Related fields and actions may join into one visual group when their relationship remains unambiguous. Icon weight, spacing and alignment should feel balanced at normal and zoomed sizes.

### Prefer quiet depth to heavy chrome

Use whitespace, tonal contrast and subtle elevation before strong outlines when the context supports it. Never rely on tint or shadow alone to identify a control or boundary. Preserve required 3:1 non-text contrast and explicit boundaries in forced-colours mode. Images may use a low-opacity outline so their edges remain clear across themes.

### Use motion as feedback

Motion should confirm an interaction or explain a state change. Keep it brief, interruptible and restrained, and respect reduced-motion preferences. Do not add motion only to make a static page feel more dynamic.

### Accessibility is the constraint, not the compromise

Every refinement must preserve or improve contrast, focus visibility, keyboard operation, touch operation, target size, zoom behaviour and screen-reader meaning. Never use softness, colour or animation as the only way to communicate state.

### Design once for every theme

Express shared capability through semantic tokens and component variants. A refinement is not complete if it only works in the default theme. Brand-specific appearance remains in the theme layer.

## Acceptance checks

Test the contexts affected by the change, including:

- All five Mangrove themes
- Keyboard, pointer and touch interaction
- Default, hover, focus, active, disabled, loading and error states where relevant
- Representative mobile, tablet and desktop widths
- 200% text sizing and 400% zoom or reflow where applicable
- Right-to-left layouts and representative translated or expanded strings
- Forced-colours mode and reduced-motion preferences

Use the <LinkTo kind="getting-started-accessibility" story="docs">accessibility guidance</LinkTo> and <LinkTo kind="contributing-build-a-component-review-checklist" story="docs">component review checklist</LinkTo> for the full requirements.

## Change evidence

For a visual refinement, include:

1. The user or system problem being solved
2. Before and after evidence at representative viewports
3. Relevant interaction and validation states
4. The themes and accessibility conditions checked
5. Why the change belongs to the component, a shared token or a specific theme
6. Compatibility, changelog and migration impact when defaults change

Before changing a system-wide default, prove the pattern on representative contexts such as a simple control, a dense form or table, and an editorial component. Familiar, stable interactions are preferable to bespoke ones unless evidence shows a real user need.
