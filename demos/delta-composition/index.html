National focal point

Recording a hazardous event

Every loss and damage record in DELTA attaches to a hazardous event: a flood, a tropical cyclone, a drought episode, an earthquake. Recording the event first, and recording it once, is what makes national totals reconcilable against the Sendai Framework monitor. This page is editorial guidance rendered with Mangrove's content classes, and it sits inside the same document as the reporting application below.

Before you start

Confirm the event is not already in the register. Duplicate events are the single largest source of double counting in national disaster loss databases, and they are far cheaper to prevent than to unpick after publication.

Guidance by record type

  • Human effects

  • Record deaths, missing persons, directly affected people, and injured or ill people separately. Do not merge categories to reach a headline figure — the Sendai indicators are computed from the disaggregated values, and a merged total cannot be decomposed later.

  • Damaged assets

  • Housing units, health facilities, educational facilities and critical infrastructure each have their own damage and destruction counts. Where a facility is damaged more than once by the same event, count it once.

  • Economic loss

  • Enter economic loss in national currency at the time of the event, with the reporting year recorded alongside. DELTA converts to constant international dollars for global reporting; entering pre-converted values makes the record impossible to audit.

Caveat found while building this. Even in mode B the heading stays in the legacy font. Mangrove never declares font-family on h2 — it sets it once on body and lets headings inherit. An inherited value loses to any declared value, in any layer, at any specificity. Layering fixes specificity fights; it does not fix declared-versus-inherited. Where a design system relies on inheritance and the legacy app declares directly, the consuming app still has to delete the legacy rule.
